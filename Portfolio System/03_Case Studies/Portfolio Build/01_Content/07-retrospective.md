# Retrospective (MANDATORY)

> **Purpose (per Experience Blueprint §2.5):** "The emotional and credibility peak of the case study, not an afterthought." This is the one section allowed to run to two paragraphs. It's the last *tracked* section in the progress nav — the narrative's actual endpoint.
> **Tone:** Honest reckoning, not a highlight reel.
> **Standing lesson from Feminist UX's Retrospective** (reread before drafting this one): finding the right *lens* matters more than wording. Several drafts on that project failed because the underlying content was wrong for the section, not because of how it was phrased. Ask directly what's not working if early drafts keep missing, rather than iterating on the same wrong angle.
> **This time, the lesson was applied proactively**: Nicole was asked directly what she wanted this section to say before any drafting started, rather than guessing first.

## Guiding Questions
1. What would you do differently if you started this rebuild today?
2. What's something you got wrong, underestimated, or learned the hard way, about the design work, or about working with AI this closely?
3. What are you genuinely proud of, and why, specifically, not generically?
4. How did this project change how you approach design, or your own process, now?

## Your Answers
Locked 2026-09-30. Three short paragraphs, no separate closing line, the piece was judged complete as written.

"Before this, I'd incessantly tinker with my portfolio without ever finishing it. Every pass chased a different look, never anything with real substance behind it. It took an enormous amount of time, and I still didn't feel proud of what I had.

Having an actual system changed that. For the first time, I had a real way to build my work in depth and actually show off the things I'm genuinely proud of.

The system itself was entirely new for me. I'd vibe-coded before, but never worked AI into something this deep. I wasn't sure it would hold up, and it did. It was approachable, malleable, and, when used well, it doesn't take over the work, it clears space for the parts I'm best at."

## Revision History

- **Nicole was asked directly what she wanted this section to say, before any draft was attempted** — a deliberate application of the lesson learned the hard way on Feminist UX's own Retrospective (seven failed drafts before asking directly worked). She gave three real threads in one message: relief at finally having a system after years of unsatisfied tinkering; pride in proving to herself that AI collaboration actually works, coming from someone who'd only vibe-coded before; and AI freeing her to focus on human judgment and connection.
- **The third thread was deliberately left out**, not by oversight. Claude flagged directly that it substantially overlaps with Constraints' own "design still needs a designer" argument, and repeating it here, even in a warmer voice, risked reading as restatement rather than new ground. Nicole agreed with this read when the first draft was built around only the first two threads.
- **The first full draft needed real fixes, not just polish**: the original closing line restated the opening problem (tinkering, wasted time) rather than landing anywhere new, and was cut. A transition between the two main beats felt disconnected and was fixed with an added bridge sentence tying the system directly to where the AI-learning actually happened.
- **The real breakthrough was Nicole's own final rewrite of the piece**, given almost verbatim here with only two typo corrections ("incessantly," "malleable") and two word-choice fixes ("part" to "parts"; "actually" removed from the final sentence). Once she wrote it herself, in her own rhythm, it read as genuinely complete without needing a separate closing line tacked on, confirmed directly by asking rather than assuming a closer was required.
