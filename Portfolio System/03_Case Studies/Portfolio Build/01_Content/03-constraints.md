# Constraints

> **Purpose:** The real limits the work had to happen inside — this is what justifies the Decisions section that follows. Constraints aren't excuses; they're the shape of the problem.
> **Standing rule (established 2026-09-29):** ground a reader in plain terms before going specific.
> **Format, established through this section's own drafting (2026-09-30)**: framed as general AI limitations, not specific site-history anecdotes — this keeps the section ahead of Decisions/Production in the linear story without needing anything from those later sections to already be established. Each card leads with a bolded anchoring sentence for skimming.

## Your Answers
Locked 2026-09-30. Opener (two short paragraphs) + 3 constraints, each bolded-anchor + explanation + closing single-clause reason + closing line.

"AI can produce endless options, fast. What it can't do is know what it actually feels like to be the person on the other side of the work. It doesn't know what's actually good in a given context, or why one version of something matters more than another.

That's the thing no prompt can replace. So I stayed hands-on with every real call, not just reviewing what came back. That real, human connection to the person reading is what drove everything here."

**Constraint 1 — narrative vs. logical order:**
"AI optimizes for what's logically correct, not what actually reads well emotionally. It can organize a portfolio soundly without understanding that a reader needs to trust the person behind it before they're ready to be impressed by the work. That distinction isn't something AI has built in. I had to bring it."

**Constraint 2 — complete vs. readable:**
"AI treats thoroughness as a virtue on its own, including every relevant detail rather than cutting for what a reader will actually get through. But completeness and readability aren't the same goal, and only one matters to someone giving a page ten seconds. Knowing which to prioritize isn't something AI decides. I had to."

**Constraint 3 — assumed shared context:**
"AI assumes a reader already knows what it knows. Left unchecked, it writes as if the reader were part of the conversation that produced the content, skipping context a stranger would need. Catching that gap isn't something AI does on its own."

**Closing line:**
"Design still needs a designer, especially when it's your own story someone's trying to connect with. My next real decisions were about keeping my judgment in control."

**Bold placement (final, locked):**
- Constraint 1: "AI optimizes for what's logically correct, not what actually reads well emotionally."
- Constraint 2: "AI treats thoroughness as a virtue on its own,"
- Constraint 3: "AI assumes a reader already knows what it knows."

## Revision History

This section required the most back-and-forth of anything drafted so far on Portfolio Build, across several genuinely distinct problems, not just wording polish:

- **Original examples were rejected wholesale, not adjusted.** The first full draft used three specific bug-catches (a wrong-tool mistake, a nav link, a stale doc). Nicole flagged these as reading like generic "AI can make mistakes, double-check your work" — true, but adding no real value to her story, since none of them required actual design judgment, just diligence.
- **A real, substantive reframe followed**: examples needed to demonstrate taste and lived experience, not error-catching. This produced a version built around real site history (a homepage comparison round, a narrative reorder, a How I Work scope pivot) — genuinely closer, but Nicole caught a real structural risk: Constraints sits before Decisions and Production in the locked spine, so referencing specific site history this early would jump the story out of order and read like content that belonged later.
- **The section was reframed a second time, more fundamentally**: written as general AI limitations rather than specific anecdotes, while still rooted in Nicole's real experience. This solved the ordering problem entirely, since general claims about AI don't require anything from later sections to already be established.
- **A real accountability moment**: Claude asserted that Nicole had said to cut a constraint entirely, when she had only said it felt repetitive. Nicole caught this and corrected it directly. Standing correction: "repetitive" means tighten, not cut, unless explicitly told to cut.
- **A fourth candidate constraint (claimed vs. actual review) was proposed and cut** by Nicole's choice, landing the section at 3, matching the pattern already set by Discovery.
- **The opener went through a real reframing of its own**: an early version leaned on "AI has no taste" in a way that read as blase/dismissive. Rewritten around lived experience and human connection instead, then further broadened after Nicole flagged that anchoring too specifically on "tired, skimming" read as narrow — broadened to lived experience and human connection generally.
- **The closing line went through roughly a dozen real iterations**, the most of any single line in this project. Early attempts ("design still needs a designer" alone, or followed by lines claiming to "prove" something already established, or narrating "everything that follows was built with that in mind") all read as either falsely vigilant, redundant, or disconnected. The breakthrough was tying the close back to the opener's own human-connection thread (rather than just restating the section's own point) and using it as a genuine bridge into Decisions' first real content (the division-of-labor / judgment-in-control decision), rather than a standalone summary sentence. An intermediate version used "judging you," which Nicole caught as undercutting the warmth of the human-connection idea; revised to "your own story someone's trying to connect with."
