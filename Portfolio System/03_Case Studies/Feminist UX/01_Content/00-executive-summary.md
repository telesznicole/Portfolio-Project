## Hero Visual
Locked 2026-08-XX. Three responsive versions: `feminist_ux_hero_desktop.png`, `feminist_ux_hero_tablet.png`, `feminist_ux_hero_phone.png` (matches Meta's own three-size hero pattern). A laptop mockup of the actual live Home page (real headline "Feminist UX." and tagline "Design for everyone." visible, real nav), set against a purple halftone dot portrait as background texture, plus a purposefully-placed cursor near the screen and the "Feminist UX" wordmark top-right.

**Real iteration history, not a first-try result:**
- **First concept (Claude's suggestion): a red halftone treatment applied to an abstract shape.** Rejected once actually mocked up, since a decorative red blob didn't carry real meaning the way the halftone treatment does elsewhere on the site (the bell hooks portrait), where it's applied to something specific and recognizable.
- **Second version (Nicole's own first real mockup): a laptop showing the five core tenets as cards, red halftone dots as background texture.** Genuinely closer, but created real redundancy with the Product Gallery component directly below the hero, which already handles "here's what's inside" — showing the tenets here meant a reader saw core content twice in quick succession.
- **Third version: dropped the tenet cards, kept real headline/tagline copy on screen instead** ("Feminist UX. Design for everyone.") — this fully resolved the Gallery redundancy, since the hero now states brand/identity, not content specifics.
- **A genuine design disagreement, resolved in Nicole's favor**: Claude initially read the cursor as purely decorative and suggested cutting it. Nicole pushed back directly — it was intentional, meant to signal the site's real interactivity. Kept, and repositioned closer to the screen in the final version so it reads as pointing at something rather than floating in empty space.
- **Color changed from red to purple**, at Nicole's call, based on her own direct knowledge of the live Framer build's actual color usage in the first few scrolls (something Claude couldn't independently verify, deferred to Nicole's judgment of her own site).
- **The halftone dot density was refined to actually form a deliberate figure** (a real portrait shape, not scattered texture) in the final version — this was the one specific quality Claude flagged as most important to get right, since scattered dots read as generic decoration while a dot-formed image ties directly to the site's established visual technique.
