# Retrospective (MANDATORY)

> **Purpose (per Experience Blueprint §2.5):** "The emotional and credibility peak of the case study, not an afterthought — the most considered part of the page, not the most compressed." This is the one section allowed to run to two paragraphs. It's the last *tracked* section in the progress nav — the narrative's actual endpoint.
> **Tone:** Honest reckoning, not a highlight reel.

## Guiding Questions
1. What would you do differently if you started this thesis today?
2. What's something you got wrong, underestimated, or learned the hard way?
3. What are you genuinely proud of, and why — specifically, not generically?
4. How did this project change how you approach design now?

## Your Answers
Locked 2026-08-XX, after the most extensive iteration of any section in this project (7+ real drafts, each rejected for a specific, different reason before landing).

"This project changed what I think I'm capable of as a designer. I've worked on plenty of collaborative work before, but I'd never carried something completely on my own: the research, the framework, the interaction design, the front-end build, and the validation, all under one person's judgment, for over a year.

Iterating on my own ideas that many times, especially the parts that changed the most, sharpened something specific: knowing when to hold a decision and when to actually let it go and rebuild. That's a judgment call I use on every project now, not just this one.

I came out of it a more confident designer, and genuinely excited about what I can take on next. Not because the work got easier, but because I proved to myself I could carry something this big all the way through, on my own judgment, start to finish."

## Revision History — genuinely the longest, most iterative revision process in this project, worth documenting honestly rather than smoothing over

This section required finding the right *lens*, not just the right wording, and got there only after several genuinely wrong turns:

1. **First draft leaned heavily on the personal reflection material already gathered earlier in this project** (the fear of diving into feminist theory, the "feminism is personal" realization). Nicole flagged this as too long to even want to read in this format.
2. **A shortened version of the same material** was flagged as adding nothing new: it read as a summary of Discovery/Decisions in a different voice, not genuine retrospective content.
3. **A version centered on internal fear/imposter feelings about not being a feminist theory expert** was flagged directly: this read as a retrospective on feminism itself, not on a product design project, and wasn't useful from a recruiter's perspective.
4. **A version reframing that same fear into a "don't wait for mastery" design lesson** was rejected outright, with no further explanation requested — the lens itself was wrong, not just the wording.
5. **A version centered on a real process moment** (the Thesis Review panel flagging the framework's foundation as too thin, requiring rework) was rejected as inaccurate to how it actually happened, and Nicole was explicit this shouldn't be forced into a negative-to-positive arc at all — it didn't need to be a mistake-and-recovery story.
6. **A purely reflective version, no imposed arc**, landed closer but was flagged as needing "some sort of uplifting micro narrative," not just a recounting of events.
7. **A version building a specific invented scene** (sitting down some days without clarity, then a moment things "clicked") was rejected as a fabricated moment — Nicole was explicit this should be about her growth as a designer, not a dramatized narrative device.
8. **The version that finally worked** dropped the fear/theory framing entirely, dropped any invented scene, and centered purely on what she can now say about herself as a designer: carrying something this large solo, sharpening real judgment through heavy iteration, coming out more confident. Nicole called this "nearly perfect" and would have accepted it as-is.
9. **One final micro-revision**: the closing line was brightened slightly, adding "genuinely excited about what I can take on next" — everything else preserved exactly, per Nicole's explicit instruction not to change anything else.

**The core lesson from this whole process, worth stating plainly for future sections**: Retrospective needed a lens shift, not incremental wording fixes. Several drafts in a row failed for different surface reasons before the real issue surfaced, that the content itself (feminism-journey material, an imposter-syndrome arc, an invented scene) was the wrong material for this section, not just imperfectly worded. When repeated revisions keep missing, the right move is to ask directly what's wrong rather than keep guessing at new angles on the same underlying material — that direct question ("what specifically isn't working") is what actually unlocked the correct direction after several failed attempts.
