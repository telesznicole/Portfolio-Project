## Your Answers
Finalized 2026-08-23, after the full-materials re-review (supersedes the 2026-08-21 version — several proposed changes were explicitly reverted mid-review, see revision history below). Structure: opener (methodology + Nicole's specific role, now with corrected scale numbers) → four short findings (card-shaped, roughly equal length) → closing reframe (now names the actual three-tier framework, with each finding mapped to one tier).

**Opener:**
"My work on this project started with research: self-ethnography, interviews with friends, and online research, on top of structured focus groups and surveys, pulling in 150+ survey responses and 300+ insights from focus group sessions alone, totaling more than 5,000 data points. I contributed across that data collection, but where I focused most was synthesis, turning all of it into affinity-mapped themes."

**Findings:**

"Public use came with real friction. Several participants described feeling self-conscious triggering voice commands like 'Hey Meta' around other people." *(Everyday Living tier)*

"People wanted the AI itself to feel less robotic. A recurring wish across interviews was for a more natural, humanized interaction, not just a faster assistant." *(Personal Identity tier)*

"Self-expression mattered more than expected, but the hardware itself was locked. With 71% seeing wearable tech as self-expression, that pushed personalization toward the experience layer instead of the product." *(Personal Identity tier)*

"Belonging mattered as much as personalization. 76% said they only shared with their inner circle, treating connection as something built in small circles, not broadcast widely." *(Belonging tier — replaces the original Finding 4 entirely, see revision history)*

**Closing reframe:**
"Across all of it, we landed on a three-tier hierarchy of what Gen Z actually wanted: for the glasses to fit seamlessly into everyday life, to create small moments of shared belonging, and to feel like a genuine extension of personal identity. That wasn't a feature gap. It was a behavior gap. People didn't need the glasses to do more, they needed a way to use them that fit naturally into how they already moved through the world."
