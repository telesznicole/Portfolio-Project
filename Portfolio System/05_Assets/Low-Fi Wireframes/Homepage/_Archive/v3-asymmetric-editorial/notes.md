# Homepage Wireframe — v3: Asymmetric Editorial

**Generated:** Claude (Sonnet), August 2026
**Relationship to prior concepts:** A genuinely new layout logic — not a revision of any round 1 or round 2 concept. Built specifically in response to "give me a few very new ones."
**Tested against:** Nicole's round 2 design review, Design Exploration/Homepage (Rashi's eclectic featured-work pattern)
**Human curation applied:** None yet — pending Nicole's review.
**Shared Components used:** Nav, footer base, and logo-placeholder pattern from `Shared Components/README.md`.

---

## What This Iteration Tests

Whether visual weight and asymmetry can communicate hierarchy more effectively than uniform blocks in a fixed order. Every prior concept has presented Work items as equally-sized units, differentiated only by their position in a sequence. This concept tests making Meta visually larger and more prominent than the other two flagship projects — signaling "this is the strongest credential" through scale, not just through going first.

## Design Rationale

This is a genuinely different composition, not a restyled version of the wide/breathable direction:

**Hero:** A 60/40 asymmetric split — headline occupies the left ~60%, with the "Trusted by" credibility lockup pushed into the negative space on the right rather than centered beneath the headline. This tests a different relationship between the two elements than `v3-integrated-hero`'s stacked treatment: here they coexist side by side, each with room, rather than one sitting quietly beneath the other.

**Work:** A masonry-style grid — Meta gets a large lead tile at nearly double the visual size of the other two, which sit stacked beside it as a smaller pair. This is a real structural difference from every prior concept's uniform feature blocks: hierarchy is expressed through size, not sequence.

**About:** An asymmetric two-column split — the pull-quote sits alone in a narrower left column, bio and quick facts occupy a wider right column, rather than stacking vertically as in every prior concept.

**Selected Collaborations:** A logo wall — five roughly-equal logo tiles in a grid, each with a one-line role beneath, rather than the horizontal row-list treatment used elsewhere. Denser and more grid-like, closer to a "wall of trust" than a list to read through.

## Strengths

- Genuinely different information hierarchy, not just a different arrangement of the same hierarchy — this directly answers the "same layout presented three times" critique from round 2.
- The Meta lead-tile treatment does real communicative work: it signals relative importance without relying on the reader inferring anything from order alone.
- Distinct enough from `v3-integrated-hero` that reviewing them side by side should produce a genuine preference, not a "these feel the same" reaction.

## Weaknesses

- Asymmetry is riskier than the wide/centered treatment — it depends more heavily on careful proportion and alignment to read as intentional rather than chaotic, and wireframe fidelity makes that harder to judge than it will be with real visual design.
- The logo wall for Selected Collaborations is denser than the row-list treatment validated in round 2 — worth checking this doesn't reintroduce some of the density concerns from `v1-compact-dashboard`, even though it's contained to one section rather than the whole page.
- The masonry Work grid is the most layout-complex pattern tested so far and has the most breakpoints to get right (asymmetric two-column → stacked) — more surface area for something to look unbalanced on an in-between viewport width.

## Research Informing This Direction

- **Nicole's round 2 design review (this session):** direct source for the request this concept answers — a genuinely different look, not a refinement.
- **Design Exploration — Homepage/Rashi:** original source for the eclectic, non-uniform featured-work arrangement this concept builds on more literally than any prior concept has.

## Who This Serves Best

**Design Directors and visually-sophisticated reviewers** who respond to considered asymmetry as a craft signal — this concept has the highest ceiling for reading as distinctive, and the highest risk of reading as unbalanced if execution isn't careful.

## Outcome

**Status: Discarded** (hero and Work sections specifically), per Nicole's round 3 design review (August 2026). **Two elements salvaged and carried forward.**

**Feedback:** "I don't mind the vertical variations of the about and selected works sections at the bottom, but I don't really like the hero and work sections. I think the variations for about and selected works could be integrated into some other variations, but I would ditch everything else."

**Salvaged:** The About two-column split (pull-quote in a narrower column, bio/facts in a wider one) and the Selected Collaborations logo-wall grid both carry forward into round 4 — specifically into `v4-asymmetric-refined`, which pairs them with the validated hero/Work treatment from `v3-integrated-hero` instead of this concept's rejected asymmetric hero and masonry Work grid. The 60/40 asymmetric hero and the masonry Work grid are both discarded outright.
