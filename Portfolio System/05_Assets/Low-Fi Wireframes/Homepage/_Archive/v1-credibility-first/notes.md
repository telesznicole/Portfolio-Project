# Homepage Wireframe — v1: Credibility-First

**Generated:** Claude (Sonnet), August 2026
**Tested against:** Experience Blueprint §2.1–2.3, Portfolio Strategy research, Stakeholder Evaluation Strategy
**Human curation applied:** None yet — first pass, pending Nicole's Tier 2 review per Wireframing Workflow §5.

---

## What This Iteration Tests

Whether leading with concrete evidence of credibility — a stat strip, a proof callout on every card, a collaborator pull-quote — builds trust faster than positioning language alone, for the specific reviewers (Hiring Managers, PMs) who are documented to scan for business/outcome framing before reading further.

## Design Rationale

This directly answers the revised V2 strategy's company-targeting correction: differentiation through method rather than sector requires the *method* to be visibly evidenced, not just claimed. Stating "systems thinking, engineering fluency, research rigor" in the hero only works if a skeptical reviewer sees it backed up almost immediately — the stat strip (sponsored engagements, an independent thesis, a CS background) and a proof callout on every flagship card exist to do that backing-up before a single word of narrative has been read.

The pull-quote in About draws on Andrea's homepage pattern from the Design Exploration library, where a collaborator's own words did real trust-building work in very little space — used here deliberately, not decoratively.

## Strengths

- Directly answers the academic-work discount risk flagged in the revised strategy — every card leads with a concrete outcome statement before the reader can mentally file the work as "just a school project."
- The stat strip does real work for a candidate breaking into their first industry role: it substitutes recognizable, verifiable numbers for a track record that isn't there yet.
- Scans nearly as fast as Concept 1, but with clearer hierarchy — proof callouts are visually distinct from card headlines, so a fast reader can catch the evidence without reading full sentences.

## Weaknesses

- The stat strip carries real risk if any of its numbers ever look inflated or thin — "7 sponsored engagements" needs to hold up to scrutiny, and a skeptical Hiring Manager may probe exactly this kind of front-loaded credibility claim harder than a plainer hero would invite.
- Proof callouts risk feeling like marketing copy if the wording drifts even slightly from plain, verifiable language — this concept has the least margin for error in tone of the five.
- The pull-quote depends entirely on having a genuinely earned, specific quote available. A generic or vague one would undercut the whole section's purpose and should not be used as a placeholder-to-real swap without real scrutiny.
- Slightly busier hero than Concept 1's already-dense version — three distinct credibility elements (headline, stats, logos) stacked before Work even starts.

## Research Informing This Direction

- **Design Exploration — Homepage/Andrea:** direct source for the pull-quote pattern.
- **Design Exploration — Homepage/Alvaro & Homepage/Rashi:** stat-strip and credibility-forward hero patterns.
- **Portfolio Strategy research & Stakeholder Evaluation Strategy:** Hiring Manager/PM persona's documented preference for business/outcome framing before narrative depth.
- **2026 Hiring Expectations research:** informs the framing around the academic-work discount this concept is built to counteract.

## Who This Serves Best

**Hiring Managers and PMs** — the persona most documented as wanting evidence and outcome framing early. Also performs well for Recruiters, since evidence density is high without requiring deep reading. Serves **Design Directors least well** — craft-sensitive reviewers may read the stat strip as slightly salesy rather than substantive.

## Outcome

**Status: Discarded**, per Nicole's design review (August 2026).

**Feedback:** "I do like the banner for the credibility here and the use of a quote, but I think that's about it. I don't like the ordering or layout at all - it feels like information is just littered across the screen with no rhyme or reason. When someone goes through my homepage, I want the information to almost seem like it's naturally answering the next question they're about to ask about me in their head - like an experiential scroll. This also feels a little too compact for me."

Two elements survive this discard and should carry forward: the credibility logo banner and the collaborator pull-quote. What didn't work was the *ordering logic* — density and information order that don't map to a natural sequence of questions a visitor would actually ask. Archived; see `v2-editorial-wide`, `v2-progressive-refined`, and `v2-hybrid-breathing-scroll`, each of which reintroduces the logo banner and pull-quote within a paced, breathing-room-led sequence instead.
