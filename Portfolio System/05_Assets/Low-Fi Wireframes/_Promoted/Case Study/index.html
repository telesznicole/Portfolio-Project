Context

This section opens the story before the work itself began. It should describe the situation as it existed prior to any involvement — who the project was for, what the original brief or ask actually was, and why the problem mattered to the people or organization behind it. A reader should finish this paragraph understanding the starting point clearly enough to feel the stakes of what comes next, without yet knowing how it gets resolved.

Discovery

This section names what was actually learned once research or exploration began — the specific insight, pattern, or reframing that wasn't obvious from the initial brief alone. It should point to a real finding, not just assert that research happened, and should make clear how that finding changed the direction the work ended up taking.

Constraints

This section names the real limits the work had to fit inside — timeline, budget, technical platform, team size, or any stakeholder requirement that shaped what was actually possible. These should be specific and checkable, not a vague gesture at "a tight timeline," since specificity here is what makes the trade-offs later in the page feel earned rather than convenient.

Retrospective

This is the most important place on the page to be honest. It should name something that didn't go perfectly — a decision made without enough time to fully test it, a scope cut that mattered, or something that would be handled differently with more time or information. This isn't a place to undercut the work; it's where the work becomes credible, because nothing before it was oversold.
