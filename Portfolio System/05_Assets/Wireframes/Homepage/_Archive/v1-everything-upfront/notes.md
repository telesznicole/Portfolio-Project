# Homepage Wireframe — v1: Everything Up Front

**Generated:** Claude (Sonnet), August 2026
**Tested against:** Experience Blueprint §2.1–2.4 (Hero, Work, About, Contact), Information Architecture §3.1
**Human curation applied:** None yet — first pass, pending Nicole's Tier 2 review per Wireframing Workflow §5.

---

## What This Iteration Tests

Whether the homepage can front-load nearly all Layer-0 content (positioning, credibility bar, quick status, all three flagship cards, and Selected Collaborations) into a short, dense scroll — minimizing the distance between arrival and evidence, rather than pacing it out.

## Design Rationale

This concept takes the "everything up front" instinct from the Design Exploration library (Abdulqahar's homepage, which surfaced status, stack, and contact simultaneously) and applies it with more restraint than the source reference — a single hero block with a clear typographic hierarchy (headline → credibility → status line) rather than competing elements fighting for attention at once. Section boundaries are marked with thin dividers rather than generous whitespace, keeping the whole page compact enough that a visitor can reach Contact without much scrolling at all.

This is a direct, literal-minded response to the calibrated funnel strategy: if the primary goal is maximizing qualified interview opportunities from a Recruiter's 6–30 second scan, the fastest way to prove that isn't burying the win condition — it's compressing the distance to it.

## Strengths

- Fastest possible path from arrival to full evidence — a Recruiter never has to scroll far to see credibility, proof, and a way to make contact.
- Nothing is buried; the three flagship cards and Selected Collaborations are both visible within the first two screens on desktop.
- Low structural complexity makes this the cheapest concept to build and maintain.

## Weaknesses

- Density this high risks the exact failure mode Abdulqahar's reference itself was flagged for in the research notes — "a tad busy." Compressing five distinct content types (headline, credibility, status, work, collaborations) into a short scroll leaves little room for any one element to breathe or register as important.
- Works against the Experience Blueprint's own pacing guidance, which explicitly calls for density to increase only after a deliberate choice to go deeper — this concept puts Work-section density in the very first viewport-and-a-half, before a visitor has done anything but arrive.
- The About and Contact sections feel like an afterthought here — compressed to match the pace of the rest of the page rather than given their own distinct rhythm, which undercuts the "turn" the Experience Blueprint describes between proof and humanization.
- Least differentiated of the five concepts. This is the safest, most template-like structural choice — it satisfies the funnel but does little to make the experience itself feel considered.

## Research Informing This Direction

- **Design Exploration — Homepage/Abdulqahar:** direct source for the "surface everything immediately" instinct, including its noted risk of feeling busy.
- **Stakeholder Evaluation Strategy:** Recruiter persona's 6–30 second scan window is the primary justification for compression.
- **Portfolio Strategy research:** supports minimizing friction between arrival and proof, though doesn't specifically endorse compressing *all* proof into the first screens.

## Who This Serves Best

**Recruiters**, unambiguously — this is the fastest-scanning concept of the five. It serves the persona the calibrated funnel strategy names as the current highest priority (per the revised V2 strategy: maximizing qualified interview opportunities). It serves Design Directors and Senior Designers worst — the density leaves little room for the "messy middle" pacing or considered craft they're documented to look for.

## Outcome

**Status: Discarded**, per Nicole's design review (August 2026).
