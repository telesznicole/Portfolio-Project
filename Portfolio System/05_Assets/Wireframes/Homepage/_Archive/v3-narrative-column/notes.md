# Homepage Wireframe — v3: Narrative Column

**Generated:** Claude (Sonnet), August 2026
**Relationship to prior concepts:** A genuinely new value proposition, not a revision — text-led rather than image-led, narrow throughout by deliberate choice rather than inconsistency.
**Tested against:** Nicole's round 2 design review ("give me a few very new ones")
**Human curation applied:** None yet — pending Nicole's review.
**Shared Components used:** Nav and footer base from `Shared Components/README.md`, logo-placeholder pattern adapted into an inline variant (see below).

---

## What This Iteration Tests

Whether a text-forward, single-narrow-column treatment — closer to a long-form essay than a series of visually distinct sections — can communicate craft and thinking as effectively as the image-forward concepts, given how research-driven and writing-heavy the actual work (particularly the Feminist UX thesis) genuinely is.

## Design Rationale

Every concept so far has treated imagery as the primary evidence and text as supporting material. This concept inverts that: the entire page is one consistent narrow column (680px), and Work entries are presented as short prose passages with a small thumbnail rather than large feature images. The width choice here is the direct, deliberate counterpart to `v3-integrated-hero`'s "one wide width for everything except hero" fix — this concept picks the opposite answer on purpose (one narrow width for the *entire* page, hero included), so reviewing them together tests a real question: does this portfolio want to look like a design showcase, or read like a considered piece of writing?

Company logos — still present per the round 2 requirement — are handled differently here to match the column's voice: small inline marks embedded directly in sentences ("Collaborated with **Meta**, **BMW**...") rather than a boxed row or wall. This keeps logos doing their recognition job without breaking the column's continuous, literary feel.

## Strengths

- The most genuinely different concept of the four — this isn't a variation on a theme, it's a different bet about what the portfolio is fundamentally showing off.
- Particularly well-suited to the Feminist UX thesis specifically, which is research-and-writing-driven — this treatment could make that project's depth more legible than a large hero image ever would.
- Simplest concept to build and maintain by a wide margin — one column, no grid logic, minimal breakpoints.

## Weaknesses

- This is the riskiest concept relative to the Experience Blueprint's own stated goal that "the projects remain primary focus, personality expressed through small details" — text-forward is a legitimate interpretation of that, but it's a real departure from every visual reference in the Design Exploration library, which skewed image-forward.
- Small thumbnails mean visual craft (an important signal for the roles this portfolio targets, per Nicole's own round 2 feedback on `v1-compact-dashboard`) is much less immediately visible than in any other concept — worth weighing carefully against that exact concern.
- Could read as underselling the work to a Recruiter doing a fast visual scan, since there's comparatively little to look at without reading.

## Research Informing This Direction

- **Nicole's round 2 design review (this session):** direct source for the request to see something genuinely different, not a refinement.
- **Feminist UX thesis content itself:** the underlying justification for testing whether a text-forward treatment might actually serve this specific project better than an image-led one.

## Who This Serves Best

**Reviewers who read closely rather than scan** — plausibly a Design Director or a reviewer specifically drawn to the Feminist UX thesis's research depth. Likely the **weakest fit for a fast-scanning Recruiter** of all four round 3 concepts — a real trade-off worth naming plainly rather than glossing over.

## Outcome

**Status: Discarded**, per Nicole's round 3 design review (August 2026). **One element salvaged.**

**Feedback:** "I think this veers too far into text... I think this is too much text and having it in a thin column reads as low effort design rather than polished. I do like the idea of maybe having the logo for the featured work next to the company name, but I think this would be in addition to whatever picture I include for it."

The narrow, text-forward premise is fully discarded — confirmed as the wrong bet. **Salvaged:** pairing a small company logo mark next to the project title within each Work card, in addition to the existing large image, not replacing it. This carries forward into every round 4 concept's Work section treatment.
