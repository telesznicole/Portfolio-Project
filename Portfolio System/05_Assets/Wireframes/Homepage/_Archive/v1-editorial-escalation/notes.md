## What This Iteration Tests

The opposite hypothesis from Concept 1: whether generous whitespace and single-idea-per-section pacing produces a stronger read, even at the cost of scan speed. Each flagship project gets its own large feature block with alternating image/text orientation, rather than a compact card grid.

## Design Rationale

This concept is a direct response to your own critique in the Design Exploration library — Belle's homepage was flagged specifically for needing more breathing room between sections, and Andrea's layout notes praised clear differentiation between content blocks. This wireframe tests what happens if that instinct is taken seriously and applied consistently: large section breaks, one idea at a time, alternating left/right feature blocks for the three flagship projects so each one gets full visual weight rather than competing in a grid.

This concept trusts the Experience Blueprint's narrative-arc framing (Arrival → Evidence → Context → Invitation) more literally than Concept 1 — each beat gets room to actually register as its own moment, rather than being compressed into a single fast scroll.

## Strengths

- Directly answers the Manifesto's instruction that the interface should never distract from the story — each project is given room to be read as a considered piece of work, not a thumbnail.
- The alternating feature-block pattern gives the Work section real visual rhythm without introducing decorative motion — this is pacing achieved through layout and spacing alone.
- Strongest fit for the "messy middle" and craft-sensitive reading that Directors and Senior Designers are documented to look for.

## Weaknesses

- This is the slowest concept to scan of all five. A Recruiter skimming in under 30 seconds may not reach Terra Dotta, About, or Contact at all without a very confident sense that scrolling further is worth their time.
- Total scroll distance is considerably longer than Concept 1 or Concept 5 — on a slow mobile connection, this concept puts more content below an initial viewport that a fast-scanning visitor may never reach.
- Requires the anchor nav to work reliably, since a Recruiter is more likely to jump directly to `#contact` or use the nav to skip ahead here than to scroll linearly — if anchor-jump navigation has any friction, this concept suffers more from it than the denser ones.
- Selected Collaborations, positioned at the end of a long Work section, risks being the least-seen content on the entire page.

## Research Informing This Direction

- **Design Exploration — Case Studies/Belle & Layout/Andrea:** direct source for the breathing-room correction this concept is built around.
- **Experience Blueprint §1 (Narrative Progression):** the arc this concept takes most literally — each beat as a distinct moment, not a blended scroll.
- **Interaction Principles research:** supports generous pacing and native scroll physics, cautions against the risk of losing a time-constrained reviewer in a longer page — the tension this concept accepts deliberately.

## Who This Serves Best

**Design Directors and Senior Designers** — the audience documented as spending 7–15 minutes and specifically evaluating craft, pacing, and "messy middle" honesty. Serves **Recruiters worst** of the five concepts; this is a real trade-off, not an oversight.
