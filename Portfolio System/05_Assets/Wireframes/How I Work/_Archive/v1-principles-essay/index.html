Process

How I Work

A closer look at how AI fits into my design process — including the process that built this site.

AI accelerates. It doesn't decide.

I use AI to speed up exploration, organize complexity, and remove repetitive work — not to make creative decisions for me. Every layout choice, every piece of writing, every cut idea on this site was mine to make and mine to defend.

The point of using AI isn't to disappear from my own work. It's to create more room for the parts of design only a human can actually do.

Every draft gets challenged before it survives.

Nothing an AI generates for this portfolio ships as-is. The loop is always the same: I provide the input, AI proposes a draft, I critique it against what I actually meant, AI revises, and only then is it done.

First passes are starting points, not finished work. The strongest results come from generating, evaluating, and refining — not accepting the first thing that looks done.

The reasoning stays, not just the result.

This entire project keeps a running decision log — not a highlight reel of what worked, but a record of what was tried, what got discarded, and why. The homepage alone went through nine rounds of iteration before a version was approved.

Every one of those rounds is still documented, not deleted, because the reasoning behind a decision is worth as much as the decision itself.

Different tools do different jobs.

Research, critique, and drafting don't come from the same place. Deep research tools handle broad market and industry analysis. A separate model handles strategy and critique. Claude handles large-context synthesis and the actual drafting and code you're looking at right now.

Keeping these separate isn't overhead — it's what keeps any one tool from being asked to do a job it isn't suited for.

I'm still the one who has to defend it.

If a decision on this site doesn't hold up when I explain why I made it, it doesn't ship — regardless of which tool proposed it first. AI is a genuinely useful collaborator. It isn't a substitute for judgment, taste, or accountability, and it was never meant to be.
