How I Work

What I actually believe about design, and where it comes from — not just claims, but where they've been tested.

UX practitioners who validated my Feminist UX framework

01

Design and engineering are the same conversation.

I started in software engineering before design, and I don't treat feasibility as a limit on creativity — I treat technical understanding as another design material. On the Meta x SCADpro engagement, that meant presenting interaction concepts fluent in both design rationale and implementation reality, not leaving the "how" for someone else to sort out later.

02

Systems matter more than screens.

I rarely evaluate a decision in isolation — I'm asking what it assumes and what happens next. Leading the Terra Dotta redesign meant mapping the full student journey before touching a single page, because a coherent system beats a polished screen that doesn't fit into one.

03

Design begins with people, not neutrality.

My Feminist UX thesis exists because "usable" isn't the same as "equitable" — it's a framework for designing with power, access, and context in mind, not assuming a neutral user who looks like everyone else. Validated with 10 UX practitioners, not just my own read on it.

04

Research exists to challenge me, not confirm me.

On the Meta engagement, that meant synthesizing 5,000+ research data points and exploring 80+ interaction concepts before committing to a direction — research as a way to be proven wrong early, not a formality on the way to an idea I'd already decided on.

05

AI amplifies. It doesn't decide.

I use AI to accelerate exploration and remove repetitive work, including on this site — but every layout, every cut idea, every word was mine to make and defend. At Hack iX, that meant using AI-assisted development to prototype fast without letting the tool make the calls that were actually mine to make.
