# Interview Story Bank

Purpose:
This document contains reusable behavioral interview stories.
When preparing interview answers, prioritize authentic examples,
adapt stories to the company and role, and use the STAR framework
(Situation, Task, Action, Result).

Core themes:
- Human-centered design
- Research-driven decision making
- Cross-functional collaboration
- Leadership in ambiguity
- Bridging design and technology
- Learning agility
- Inclusive design
- Communication and storytelling


# Tell Me About Yoursaelf

Core Answer:

Nicole Telesz is a UX, interaction, and product designer with a
technical foundation in computer science and a passion for designing
technology that feels more human.

She began her career in software and engineering, but wanted to work
closer to the people impacted by technology, which led her to UX and
interaction design. Her work focuses on understanding human needs,
translating insights into experiences, and bridging the gap between
design and development.

Through SCAD, she has collaborated with organizations including Meta,
FINRA, Deloitte, Terra Dotta, BMW, and Hyundai MOBIS, working on
projects involving emerging technology, accessibility, research, and
human-centered design.

Her MFA thesis, Feminist UX, represents her design philosophy by
exploring how designers can create more equitable and inclusive
experiences through intentional frameworks and practices.

Key themes:
- Designer + developer identity
- Human-centered problem solving
- Research-driven design
- Cross-functional collaboration
- Emerging technology

# STORY 1: NAVIGATING AMBIGUITY + STAKEHOLDER ALIGNMENT

Project:
Meta — Ray-Ban Meta Smart Glasses Interaction Design

Story Name:
"The Meta Alignment Reset"

Situation:
Worked on a highly open-ended blue-sky design challenge exploring
future social experiences for Ray-Ban Meta smart glasses.

The project involved ambiguity, multiple stakeholders, and competing
expectations between academic requirements, team direction, and client
goals.

Task:
Help maintain alignment and ensure design decisions remained grounded
in user needs and client objectives.

Action:
- Created shared design anchors around the client goals and design
  direction.
- Grounded decisions in research, rationale, and user-centered thinking.
- Used storytelling to communicate the reasoning behind concepts.
- Encouraged productive critique and iteration within the team.

Result:
- Improved team alignment and confidence navigating ambiguity.
- Strengthened ability to communicate and defend design decisions.
- Presented design strategy directly to Meta stakeholders at Meta HQ.

Demonstrates:
- Working through ambiguity
- Stakeholder communication
- Design rationale
- Collaboration
- Strategic thinking

Can answer:
- Tell me about a time you handled ambiguity.
- Tell me about a difficult project.
- How do you handle conflicting feedback?
- Describe a time you influenced a team.

"Owing the Hackathon Mistake"

Task:
Resolve the issue quickly while maintaining a positive participant
experience.

Action:

- Documented improvements for future planning.

Result:

- Developed stronger leadership instincts in high-pressure situations.

Demonstrates:

- Adaptability
- Leadership
- Problem solving
