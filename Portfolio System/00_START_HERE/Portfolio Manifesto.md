# Design Principles

> *This document is the foundation for every decision made within my portfolio and, more broadly, every product I design. These principles are not aspirations—they are standards. If a design decision conflicts with one of them, the design should change.*

---

# Design Begins With People

I didn't leave software engineering because I stopped enjoying code.

I left because I wanted to shorten the distance between what I build and the people I build it for.

Engineering taught me how to solve problems.

Design taught me which problems are worth solving.

Every product I create begins with people—not because it's good practice, but because technology only has value when it meaningfully improves someone's life.

If I ever find myself designing to satisfy my own preferences before understanding the people I'm designing for, I've already lost sight of the goal.

---

# Technology Should Expand People's Lives

Technology should help people do more of what already matters to them.

It shouldn't create unnecessary dependency.

The best technology quietly removes friction from people's lives.

---

# Good UX Should Be Invisible

I want people to accomplish what they came to do without extra mental burden.

People shouldn't have to learn how my product works.

My product should work the way people naturally think.

Design should feel like a continuation of someone's mental model—not a challenge to overcome.

---

# Originality Is Not Novelty

I'm not interested in creating something different simply for the sake of being different.

Meaningful ideas don't fade quickly.

The work that excites me most begins with a genuinely interesting problem and pursues a solution that makes people wonder why it didn't exist already.

I want my work to move products forward, not simply rearrange what already exists.

---

# Systems Matter More Than Screens

Individual screens are easy.

Building a coherent system is difficult.

The best products aren't collections of polished interfaces—they're collections of thoughtful decisions that reinforce one another.

I naturally think in systems.

Patterns.

Relationships.

Dependencies.

Context.

That means I rarely evaluate a decision in isolation.

I'm constantly asking:

How does this affect everything else?

What assumptions does this create?

What happens next?

Design doesn't stop at the edge of the screen.

---

# Design and Engineering Are the Same Conversation

I've lived on both sides.

Engineering and design often speak different languages, but they're solving the same problem.

Understanding both changes the questions I ask.

I don't see feasibility as something that limits creativity.

I see technical understanding as another design material.

The strongest products emerge when thoughtful design and thoughtful engineering work together—not when one compromises for the other.

---

# Research Exists to Challenge Me

Research is not there to validate my ideas.

It's there to prove me wrong before users have to experience those mistakes.

Every assumption deserves to be questioned.

Every insight should make the product better.

Research isn't a step in the process.

It's a mindset of humility.

---

# Details Communicate Care

People often describe me as detail-oriented.

I don't think that's because I like perfection.

It's because details tell people whether something was built with care.

Consistency matters.

Typography matters.

Spacing matters.

Motion matters.

Writing matters.

Tiny inconsistencies accumulate.

Thoughtful details do too.

People may never consciously notice them.

They still feel them.

---

# Feedback Is Something to Synthesize

Design isn't a performance.

Critique isn't an attack.

I've never believed the goal of feedback is to defend my work.

The goal is to understand what someone is actually trying to tell me—even if they don't have the vocabulary to express it.

Everyone speaks a different language.

One of my strengths is translating those languages into better design decisions.

---

# AI Should Amplify Human Creativity

AI is an incredible collaborator.

It is not a replacement for human thought.

I want AI to remove repetitive work.

To accelerate exploration.

To organize complexity.

To challenge my thinking.

I do not want it making creative decisions on my behalf.

The designer should still be recognizable in the final work.

The point of using AI isn't to become invisible.

It's to create more space for the parts of design that only humans can do.

---

# Accessibility Is Part of Good Design

Accessibility isn't a checklist.

It isn't an edge case.

If a product excludes people because they weren't considered during the design process, then the design is incomplete.

Designing for people means designing for all of the people who will use the product—not just the easiest ones to imagine.

---

# Build Things Worth Caring About

The projects that consume my attention are never the ones with the fanciest visuals.

They're the ones with ideas that deserve to exist.

I want to spend my career working on products that make people think:

*"I'm glad someone finally made this."*

Not because they're surprising.

Because they genuinely improve the way people live, think, create, or connect.

---

# The Standard I Hold Myself To

I never want my work to feel like a first draft, not because perfection exists but because every project deserves my best thinking.

The goal isn't flawless work. The goal is thoughtful work.

Work that reflects curiosity and care.

Work that makes people's lives a little easier than they were before.
