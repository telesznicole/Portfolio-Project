# Product Vision

## Purpose

Create a portfolio that demonstrates how I think as a product designer—not just what I have designed.

The portfolio itself should feel like one of my projects: intentionally researched, thoughtfully structured, and carefully crafted. Every interaction, layout decision, and piece of content should reinforce my approach to design.

Rather than acting as a gallery of projects, the portfolio should communicate my decision-making, systems thinking, and ability to connect research, strategy, design, and implementation.

---

## Experience Goals

Someone exploring my portfolio should leave with these impressions:

* This designer is thoughtful and intentional.
* Every detail feels purposeful.
* The work is credible and supported by evidence.
* The portfolio is easy to navigate without sacrificing depth.
* The experience feels polished without feeling overdesigned.
* The projects—not the interface—remain the primary focus.
* This feels different from a template without trying to be different.

---

## Visual Direction

The visual language should feel clean, intentional, and cohesive, providing a consistent foundation across the entire portfolio while allowing each project's visual identity to take center stage.

Personal branding should remain minimal so that the work—not the portfolio itself—is the primary focus.

The design should emphasize:

* clean layouts
* strong, bold typography
* generous spacing
* intentional use of full-bleed imagery where it strengthens the story
* visual consistency across every page

The portfolio should create hierarchy and interest through thoughtful composition, typography, and rhythm rather than heavy branding or decorative elements.

It should feel polished without becoming sterile, and expressive without becoming distracting.

---

## Personality

The portfolio should have a distinct personality that feels authentic to me.

That personality shouldn't come from loud branding or visual gimmicks—it should emerge through thoughtful, unexpected details woven throughout the experience.

Unexpected objects, subtle movement, playful interactions, and memorable moments should make the portfolio feel distinctly mine while never competing with the work itself.

The goal isn't for visitors to remember an animation or interaction.

It's for them to remember the feeling that the portfolio was thoughtfully crafted by someone who genuinely cares about the experience.

Professionalism and personality should coexist rather than compete.

---

## Communication & Credibility

The portfolio should establish credibility quickly.

Visitors should understand who I am, what I do, and why they should continue exploring within the first moments of arriving.

Rather than making claims about my abilities, the portfolio should consistently demonstrate them through:

* measurable outcomes and impact
* collaborations with recognized organizations
* awards and notable achievements where relevant
* thoughtful process and decision-making
* technical implementation where appropriate
* clear, intentional writing
* thoughtful interaction design
* careful attention to detail

Every section should reinforce the impression that my work is intentional, well-researched, and grounded in real outcomes. Trust should be built through evidence, not assertions.

---

## Philosophy

My design philosophy shouldn't be hidden on an About page—it should be reflected throughout the entire experience.

Visitors should quickly understand that I approach design as a systems thinker who values research, intentionality, craftsmanship, and thoughtful execution.

My engineering background should strengthen the story by demonstrating how technical understanding informs my design decisions, rather than becoming the defining focus of my identity.

Throughout the portfolio, the work should consistently reflect the principles that guide my practice:

* Design should reduce friction rather than create it.
* Systems should feel coherent.
* Details should communicate care.
* Research should inform decisions.
* The interface should never distract from the story being told.

---

## Success

If successful, the portfolio will not simply convince someone that I can design products.

It will allow them to experience how I think about products.
